# Yellow Researcher Opportunity Brief

**Decision: PIVOT**  
**Research date: 11 August 2026**  
**Confidence: Moderate on problem and prototype feasibility; low-to-moderate on UK/Ireland commercial magnitude.**

## 1. Executive finding

Pivot from a broad AI home-gym architect or combined room-fit and cross-brand compatibility advisor to a **retailer-owned, consumer-used, same-brand rack upgrade advisor**. The first bounded job is: given an exact rack family, model/version, height and configuration, identify which manufacturer-approved attachments are compatible, which have explicit installation conditions, which are incompatible, and which remain unknown, using a controlled catalogue with source provenance.

The problem is real. Mirafit publishes a large M4 attachment-by-rack matrix with qualifications such as rack height, stabiliser feet, floor/wall fixing and reduced workout area [2]. REP states that even nominally similar 47-inch racks cannot be assumed compatible because hole patterns, metric dimensions, height and coating thickness vary [4]. Most importantly, Bells of Steel states that attachments not fitting another brand's rack are one of its top return reasons [5]. This is direct manufacturer evidence of commercial harm, although it gives no rate, cost or UK/Ireland breakdown.

The dossier's preferred combined "Verified Fit & Compatibility" direction is too broad for the first release. Ordinary commerce data can supply products, prices, stock and basic dimensions, but the sampled standard schemas do not supply complete exercise envelopes, structural tolerances or manufacturer-approved cross-brand safety relationships [11][13][15]. Existing products also make generic room planning a crowded entry point: Rogue provides ZEUS, and Planner 5D sells a white-label gym planning and commerce platform [7][8].

This is a **PIVOT**, not a rejection of the underlying problem. The defensible opening is post-purchase ecosystem expansion with exact identity and first-party confirmation. Whole-room planning, first-purchase recommendation and cross-brand matching should remain later hypotheses.

## 2. Problem evidence

### Verified facts

- Compatibility is conditional and model-specific. Mirafit's M4 matrix contains yes/no pairings and conditions tied to rack form, height, anchoring, stabilisation, orientation and interference [2].
- Headline rack dimensions are not sufficient for installation. Mirafit's M4 rack has several heights and depths, distinguishes dimensions with and without feet, and must be bolted down with fixings appropriate to the floor [3].
- Same nominal geometry does not prove compatibility. REP refuses to guarantee Ares 2.0 fit outside specified PR-4000/PR-5000 configurations and identifies multiple sources of dimensional variation [4]. Mirafit separately warns that even if third-party attachment holes line up, use may be unsafe [1].
- Compatibility failure has a commercial consequence. Bells of Steel calls cross-brand non-fit one of its top reasons for returns [5]. Mirafit's unwanted-return policy places return postage on the customer and requires items to be unused, unbuilt and in original packaging, making late discovery materially inconvenient [1].
- A detailed matrix does not eliminate the decision burden. Mirafit's matrix is extensive and footnoted; this is evidence that the underlying knowledge can be structured, but also that a simple family label is inadequate [2].

### Inference

Owners who already know their rack are the strongest initial user group because exact product identity removes one major source of uncertainty, and attachments create repeat purchase opportunities. A seller can plausibly value fewer compatibility contacts and returns plus higher appropriate attachment sales. Those effects have not been quantified in the UK or Ireland.

### Anecdotal evidence

The supplied dossier cites home-gym forums and specialist compatibility lists. These establish vocabulary and repeated questions, not prevalence or willingness to pay. They are not used here to size the opportunity.

### Unknowns

- The proportion and cost of UK/Ireland support contacts, cancellations and returns caused by fit or compatibility.
- Whether same-brand attachment uncertainty is a top-five retailer problem or mainly an enthusiast frustration.
- Whether retailers hold exact rack versions and compatibility relationships in structured systems, documents, staff knowledge, or not at all.
- Whether a guided decision changes purchase, attachment rate or support demand compared with a good compatibility matrix.

## 3. Priority audience and current behaviour

**Priority end user:** UK/Ireland home-gym owners who already own, or have selected, a modular rack and are considering a same-brand attachment or upgrade. They need certainty about exact model/version fit, required preconditions and limitations before buying.

**Economic buyer:** rack manufacturers and specialist retailers with modular attachment catalogues. They own the authoritative relationship data, bear support and return costs, and can measure attachment conversion and return reasons.

**Current behaviour, verified or directly observable:**

- Users consult product pages, FAQs and compatibility matrices. Mirafit directs customers to its product descriptions and M4 guide and recommends against third-party rack attachments [1][2].
- When uncertainty remains, manufacturers direct users to support. Bells asks customers to provide rack details; REP asks owners to contact support when uncertain [4][5].
- Sellers already package compatible equipment. McSport's Irish catalogue includes a rack-and-pulley bundle and publishes core rack specifications, showing that retailers can merchandise ecosystems even where a public compatibility registry is not evident [9].
- The broader B2C home-gym planning job has free or commercially mature substitutes, including Rogue ZEUS and Planner 5D [7][8].

### Commercial model comparison

| Model | Evidence-based assessment | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B2C paid planner | Episodic need, free substitutes, no demonstrated willingness to pay. | Do not lead. |
| B2B internal support tool | Clear operational user and controlled data, but customer value is less visible in a public demonstration. | Secondary use. |
| B2B2C retailer-owned advisor | Consumer receives a concrete verified upgrade result; seller controls data and can measure sales/support outcomes. | Priority. |
| Independent affiliate comparison | Potential referral revenue, but permission, version maintenance and cross-brand safety burden are high. | Defer. |

The live demonstrator should query a small external controlled registry at runtime. Its compatibility records need exact product/version keys, status, conditions, source URL, source date, reviewer and last-verified date. Live price/stock may be added only from an authorized commerce source and must not imply that compatibility was verified live from that source.

## 7. Recommended opportunity and why it wins

### Approved opportunity

A **same-brand rack upgrade advisor for specialist retailers/manufacturers**, used by existing rack owners. It accepts an exact rack identity and constraints, then returns a tangible upgrade shortlist containing only manufacturer-confirmed pairings, explicit conditions and incompatible/unknown outcomes from a controlled live registry.

### Why it wins

1. **Direct category evidence:** Bells identifies non-fitting cross-brand attachments as a top return reason [5]; Mirafit and REP both publish warnings that prevent dimensional equivalence from becoming a safety claim [1][4].
2. **Bounded truth:** exact rack family/version plus same-brand relationships can be represented from manufacturer matrices and conditions [2][4].
3. **Commercial alignment:** a seller can measure appropriate attachment sales, support contacts and compatibility-coded returns. This is an inference to test, not a proven ROI claim.
4. **Differentiation:** it is not another generic room planner, where Rogue and Planner 5D already compete [7][8]. Its core value is provenance and refusal to overclaim.
5. **Prototype credibility:** a controlled external registry can be queried live and produce a concrete customer decision without unauthorized web crawling [16].

### Explicit exclusions for the first opportunity

- No cross-brand item may be labelled compatible without explicit manufacturer confirmation of the exact pairing.
- No structural safety, load-capacity, floor anchoring or movement-clearance claim may be inferred from approximate dimensions.
- No whole-room layout, training prescription or generic conversational assistant is required to prove the opportunity.
- No Mirafit branding or catalogue should be used publicly without permission; Mirafit is a benchmark and possible partner hypothesis only.

## 9. Deep Research audit

### Findings independently corroborated

- Equipment compatibility is multidimensional and version-sensitive [1][2][4][5][6].
- Mirafit's detailed catalogue is technically attractive but does not prove Mirafit has the greatest unmet need [1][2][3].
- Generic room planning is already served by specialist and horizontal products [7][8].
- Controlled, authorized product data is more defensible than unrestricted scraping [11]-[17].
- A consumer subscription is weak relative to a seller-funded B2B2C model; this remains an inference because no willingness-to-pay study was found.

### Findings disputed or insufficiently supported

- **Dossier claim: no gym-specific return evidence was found.** Disputed in part. Bells of Steel explicitly says cross-brand attachment non-fit is one of its top return reasons [5]. The dossier cited that page but did not elevate this consequential statement. Rate, cost and UK/Ireland relevance remain unknown.
- **Dossier winner: combined room fit and compatibility, 29/30.** The score overstates feasibility and data access. Mirafit publishes installed dimensions but not a universal movement envelope, and standard commerce APIs do not provide the tolerances or approved relationships needed for broad compatibility [3][11][13][15].
- **Dossier Shopify characterization:** participation/token setup was presented as required for product listings. Current Shopify 2026-04 documentation allows tokenless access to essential products and collections; tokens are still required for specialist metafields/metaobjects [11][12]. This improves basic live-catalogue feasibility but does not solve compatibility data.
- **Dossier differentiation claim:** a combined verified advisor was treated as strongly differentiated. Planner 5D now explicitly markets white-label gym planning, real products, rule-based configuration, live prices and cart/quote handoff [8]. Provenance-based compatibility remains differentiated; room planning does not.
- **Dossier numeric precision:** equal-weight scores such as 29/30 are judgments, not validation. With no buyer interviews, incidence or ROI data, they should not be read as high-confidence measurement.

### Important gaps or biases

- UK-heavy evidence and almost no Ireland-specific customer or retailer operations data. McSport confirms a relevant Irish specialist catalogue, not demand magnitude [9].
- Manufacturer documentation proves complexity and reflects manufacturer incentives; it does not reveal unbiased consumer prevalence.
- Community sources overrepresent enthusiasts and cannot size a mainstream market.
- The dossier bundled multiple claims under citations that sometimes support only part of the sentence, and its source register contains duplicated links and encoding corruption. This weakens auditability even where the underlying sources are sound.
- No interviews, support-ticket taxonomy, return reason data, conversion experiment or willingness-to-pay evidence was supplied.
- The dossier focused on recommendation delivery more than the upstream data-governance cost. A compatibility service is only as trustworthy as model identity, ownership and change control.

### Additional independent research performed

- Rechecked current official documentation from Mirafit, REP and Bells of Steel, including Mirafit's direct safety warning and Bells' return-reason statement [1][4][5].
- Compared Irish retailer McSport and mature spatial/configuration alternatives Rogue and Planner 5D [7][8][9].
- Audited current Shopify 2026-04 access behavior and metafield requirements, correcting the dossier's blanket token assumption [11][12].
- Compared WooCommerce, Akeneo, Google Merchant, Google Sheets and Awin to separate volatile commerce fields from governed compatibility truth [13]-[17].
- Reviewed general spec-comparison evidence and retained it only as directional secondary evidence [10].

### Material difference from the dossier

The dossier recommends a single-retailer advisor combining room constraints, exact product data and compatibility. This brief **pivots to post-purchase, exact-version, same-brand rack upgrades only**. Room planning is deferred because competition is mature and authoritative operating-clearance data is sparse. Cross-brand fit is deferred because the strongest primary sources explicitly reject dimension-only certainty. The new scope preserves the dossier's strongest insight, provenance-aware compatibility, while removing its least supported breadth.

## 10. Clear design implications for the Designer

These are information and trust requirements, not interface prescriptions.

- The opportunity begins with an exact owned rack identity: manufacturer, family, model/version, height and relevant configuration. Ambiguous identity must produce an unresolved outcome.
- Every compatibility result must carry one of four evidence states: **manufacturer-confirmed**, **known incompatible**, **condition-dependent**, or **unknown/review required**. Do not create a generic "likely compatible" safety state.
- Preserve the exact source, source date, last-verified date and applicable conditions for each result.
- Separate compatibility status from live price and stock. Commercial freshness must never be presented as evidence freshness.
- The tangible customer output must state the exact rack considered, approved attachment options, required conditions/limitations, exclusions and unresolved questions.
- The system must be useful when it refuses: unknown, ambiguous or cross-brand cases should retain the reason and the evidence needed for resolution.
- Avoid collecting room photos, addresses or health/training data for this first opportunity; they are unnecessary to the validated job.
- The demonstration catalogue should be deliberately small enough for every pairing to be manually audited. Breadth is not evidence quality.

## Handoff block

**Approved opportunity:** A retailer/manufacturer-owned, consumer-used same-brand rack upgrade advisor that uses a controlled live registry to return manufacturer-confirmed attachments, conditions, incompatibilities and unknowns for an exact rack model/version.

**Priority audience:** UK/Ireland owners of modular home-gym racks considering a same-brand attachment; economic buyer is the specialist retailer or manufacturer that owns compatibility truth.

**Validated needs:** Exact model/version identification; authoritative attachment pairing; explicit installation conditions and limitations; separation of verified, incompatible and unknown outcomes; current price/stock kept distinct from compatibility evidence.

**Constraints:** Same-brand first; no dimension-only safety claims; no unrestricted scraping; no public use of a company's catalogue or brand without permission; every compatibility record requires provenance and review metadata; human escalation for ambiguity.

**Evidence that must be preserved:** Mirafit's conditional compatibility matrix and cross-brand safety warning [1][2]; REP's rejection of nominal-dimension equivalence [4]; Bells of Steel's statement that cross-brand non-fit is a top return reason [5]; standard commerce feeds do not by themselves establish compatibility [11][13][15].

**Unresolved questions:** UK/Ireland incidence and cost; retailer buyer urgency; internal data ownership and version quality; whether verified guidance changes attachment conversion, support demand or compatibility-coded returns; whether one ecosystem contains enough decisions for a meaningful pilot.
