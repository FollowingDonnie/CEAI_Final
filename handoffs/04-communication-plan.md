# Green Communicator Communication Plan

**Stage:** 4 - Communicate  
**Product:** Northstar Rack Upgrade Advisor  
**Commercial posture:** Pilot-oriented fictional proof of concept for specialist rack retailers and manufacturers. It is not affiliated with Mirafit or any other real equipment company, is not a production service, and must not be presented as equipment safety advice.

## 1. Positioning statement

For specialist gym-equipment retailers and manufacturers with modular rack ecosystems, **Northstar Rack Upgrade Advisor** demonstrates how governed compatibility records can become a clear, customer-facing upgrade decision. An owner selects the exact same-brand rack version and configuration; the advisor returns a printable sheet that separates confirmed, conditional, incompatible, and unresolved outcomes and shows the evidence behind each one.

Unlike a generic chatbot, room planner, or dimension-matching tool, the proof of concept does not infer fit from similarity. Its deterministic decision flow uses a controlled registry, keeps compatibility separate from price and stock, and refuses to make a current claim when evidence is stale, contradictory, missing, cross-brand, or unavailable. The AI Decision Guide explains retrieved records but does not decide compatibility.

The commercial proposition is a **testable hypothesis**, not a promised return: a retailer-owned advisor may make same-brand upgrade decisions easier for customers and may help the seller improve appropriate attachment purchasing while reducing avoidable compatibility questions or returns. Buyer interviews, operational data, and a controlled pilot are required to establish whether those effects exist and are material.

## 2. Priority audience and message hierarchy

### Priority economic buyer

The primary buyer is a UK or Ireland specialist retailer or manufacturer that:

- sells modular racks and a meaningful same-brand attachment catalogue;
- owns or can govern exact rack-version and attachment relationships;
- receives compatibility questions or can identify compatibility-related return reasons;
- can appoint a catalogue or product owner to maintain evidence and review dates; and
- can measure customer and operational outcomes during a bounded pilot.

Relevant buyer roles are ecommerce/product leadership, customer-support leadership, catalogue/PIM ownership, product compliance or technical product ownership, and commercial leadership responsible for attachment growth.

### Priority end user

The end user is an existing rack owner, or a customer who has already selected a rack, considering a same-brand attachment. They need to identify their exact rack version, understand any explicit conditions, see unresolved evidence honestly, and retain a useful decision record.

### Message hierarchy

1. **Make the decision traceable:** Turn exact rack identity and governed relationship records into a readable Upgrade Decision Sheet.
2. **Keep uncertainty useful:** Show confirmed, condition-dependent, incompatible, and unknown outcomes instead of collapsing them into a yes/no recommendation.
3. **Protect the evidence boundary:** Do not infer compatibility from dimensions, family names, AI memory, or cross-brand similarity.
4. **Use live governed data:** Retrieve the external registry for each lookup and explanation; fail closed if it cannot be validated.
5. **Separate two kinds of freshness:** Compatibility evidence and commerce information have independent sources and dates.
6. **Test commercial value in a pilot:** Measure whether the advisor changes customer completion, appropriate product handoff, support demand, and compatibility-coded returns. Do not promise improvement in advance.

### Supporting category evidence

- Manufacturer documentation reviewed by the Researcher shows that rack compatibility can depend on exact model, height, anchoring, stabilisation, orientation, interference, hole patterns, metric dimensions, and other version-sensitive details.
- Bells of Steel states that cross-brand attachments not fitting are one of its top return reasons. This supports the claim that compatibility errors can have commercial consequences, but it does **not** quantify incidence or cost in the UK/Ireland and does not prove same-brand pilot impact.
- Mirafit and REP documentation support the need for exact, source-grounded decisions and warn against assuming compatibility from nominal geometry. Mirafit is a research benchmark only; this product has no Mirafit affiliation.

## 5. Three-to-five-minute live demonstration script

### Reproducible public setup

- Customer prototype: https://followingdonnie.github.io/CEAI_Final/
- Public API base: https://northstar-rack-advisor-api.onrender.com
- Controlled fictional registry view: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1SR78Cb75bBbrImALyUxHrwJCUy6SLJ9RDl0f4yvVTuE/edit
- Controlled CSV used at runtime: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1SR78Cb75bBbrImALyUxHrwJCUy6SLJ9RDl0f4yvVTuE/export?format=csv&gid=453706521

Use a current desktop or mobile browser with network access. The hosted backend may need a short wake-up period. Do not edit the public registry during an external demonstration. State before starting: “Everything you are about to see uses fictional Northstar equipment and is not real compatibility or safety advice.”

### Script and timing

**0:00-0:30 - Frame the buyer problem**

“Rack upgrades are not always a family-name lookup. Manufacturer documentation shows that exact versions and installation conditions can matter, while similar dimensions do not establish an approved fit. Today I am showing a fictional same-brand proof of concept: how a retailer could present its own governed compatibility knowledge as a customer decision.”

**0:30-1:15 - Create an exact assessment**

1. Open the customer prototype.
2. Select **Atlas**.
3. Select **Atlas Modular Rack V2**.
4. Select **220 cm** and answer the displayed structured stabilisation question.
5. Keep the category on **all upgrades** and create the decision sheet.

“The advisor asks structured questions because the exact identity and declared configuration drive the lookup. It is not reading dimensions and guessing.”

Point out that the sheet contains eight results and all four states: manufacturer-confirmed, condition-dependent, known incompatible, and unknown/review required.

**1:15-2:05 - Show conditions without false certainty**

1. Find **Cable Tower**.
2. Set each of its two required conditions to **Not sure** in turn, then set both to **Met**.
3. Point out the change in readiness and product-handoff visibility.
4. Point out that the evidence label remains **Condition-dependent**.

“Customer answers can show whether stated conditions appear satisfied, but they cannot promote a conditional record into a manufacturer-confirmed one. The evidence state and the customer's readiness are deliberately separate.”

**2:05-2:40 - Demonstrate version sensitivity**

1. Choose **Edit rack**.
2. Change the exact model to **Atlas Modular Rack V1**.
3. Re-run the sheet and show the changed outcomes and revision note.

“The adjacent version produces materially different results. That is the reason this experience starts with exact product identity rather than a broad chat prompt.”

**2:40-3:20 - Show provenance and the two clocks**

1. Open evidence details on one result.
2. Point out source date, compatibility check time, review due date, and record owner.
3. Point out the separately dated commerce status.

“Compatibility evidence and commerce information answer different questions. A fresh stock timestamp cannot make a compatibility record current, and a confirmed pairing does not promise that an item is available.”

**3:20-4:05 - Show bounded AI and refusal**

1. Return to Atlas V2 if needed and open **Explain this result** for Cable Tower.
2. Ask why it is condition-dependent.
3. Show the customer-facing explanation and unchanged deterministic state.
4. Ask the guide to confirm a cross-brand fit.
5. Show the scope refusal.

“The guide explains only freshly retrieved context. It cannot write records, change statuses, unlock a product handoff, or answer a cross-brand fit question.”

If the AI service is unavailable, use that as the designed fallback: show that the deterministic sheet and direct evidence remain usable.

**4:05-4:35 - Leave a tangible output**

1. Select **Print or save decision sheet**.
2. In print preview, confirm exact identity, assessment time, states, conditions, provenance, and scope note are present.
3. Cancel print unless a local copy is useful for the meeting.

“The output is a revisable, support-ready record rather than a chat transcript.”

**4:35-5:00 - Make the pilot ask**

“This demonstration proves the public flow with fictional data; it does not prove sales or return impact. The next step is a bounded pilot using one authorised same-brand catalogue, a named compatibility owner, and agreed baseline measures.”

### Demonstration recovery notes

- If the registry cannot be reached, show the retained selections, Retry action, and absence of current positive claims. This is intended fail-closed behavior.
- If the Guide cannot answer, show direct evidence and the unchanged deterministic sheet. Do not claim the AI response is guaranteed.
- If a product handoff opens a `.invalid` address, explain that purchase destinations are deliberately non-real in the fictional dataset.
- Do not demonstrate a registry edit in a buyer meeting unless the registry owner has approved a controlled test and rollback. The verified build already established that an external relationship change affects the next decision without an application rebuild.

## 7. One-page launch narrative

### Exact rack, current evidence, useful answer

Owners of modular gym racks often expand them over time. The decision looks simple until versions, heights, stabilisation, anchoring, orientation, and interference conditions enter the picture. Manufacturer documentation reviewed for this opportunity shows that compatibility can be conditional and version-specific, and that nominally similar geometry is not enough to establish an approved fit.

Northstar Rack Upgrade Advisor demonstrates a narrower way to help. A customer identifies an exact fictional Northstar rack, answers only the configuration questions required by the registry, and receives an Upgrade Decision Sheet. Every in-scope attachment appears in one of four evidence states: manufacturer-confirmed, condition-dependent, known incompatible, or unknown/review required. Unknown remains useful: it explains why no claim can be made and what evidence or action is needed next.

The system treats compatibility as governed product knowledge, not an AI opinion. A deterministic engine evaluates the retrieved record. Stale, contradictory, malformed, missing-source, cross-brand, or unavailable evidence can only lower confidence; it cannot create a positive claim. Conditions can change customer readiness but cannot rewrite the underlying evidence state. Price and stock are displayed separately because commerce freshness is not compatibility truth.

The optional Decision Guide has a deliberately small job. It can explain the current retrieved record in customer language and point to evidence already present on the sheet. It cannot determine fit, alter conditions, change status, create a purchase action, or answer broad and cross-brand questions. If AI is unavailable, the decision sheet remains usable. If the live registry is unavailable, the advisor retains the customer's inputs but suppresses current compatibility claims.

The public experience uses fictional Northstar Demonstration Equipment records and reserved non-real product links. It proves that the flow can operate across a public frontend, backend, and externally controlled registry; that exact rack selection changes results; that four evidence states and conditional readiness work; that explanations remain bounded; and that the result can be printed. It does not prove real equipment compatibility, safety, production readiness, retailer demand, lower returns, lower support demand, or higher sales.

The next commercial step is therefore not a broad launch. It is a partner discovery and pilot-readiness phase with specialist UK/Ireland retailers or manufacturers. A suitable partner must be able to provide authoritative same-brand records, name an owner, maintain review dates, approve customer wording, and measure a baseline. A first pilot should cover one rack family and a bounded set of attachments, run alongside existing support, and stop if data ownership, evidence quality, safe escalation, or buyer urgency is inadequate.

Success would mean learning with evidence: whether customers can identify the right rack, whether the sheet helps them complete an upgrade decision, how often evidence remains unresolved, whether support work changes, and whether appropriate attachment handoffs or compatibility-coded returns move against the agreed baseline. Only those measured findings could support a later commercial claim.

## 9. Recommended adoption or pilot journey

### Phase 0 - Qualification and baseline

- Interview ecommerce, support, product-data, and technical/compliance owners.
- Confirm that compatibility is material enough to test and that the partner can categorise relevant contacts and returns.
- Select one same-brand rack family with exact version identifiers and a bounded attachment set.
- Record the current journey: matrix/product-page use, escalation route, completion, support contacts, attachment conversion where measurable, and compatibility-coded returns.
- Define success and stop criteria before changing the customer experience. Do not invent universal target percentages.

**Gate:** Stop if the seller reports trivial operational/customer impact, cannot identify authoritative truth, or cannot name an owner and review process.

### Phase 1 - Data-readiness audit

- Map exact rack versions, active and legacy identities, attachments, conditions, negative relationships, unknowns, sources, reviewers, and review due dates.
- Separate compatibility records from price, stock, and product-link records.
- Sample records with technical/product owners and reconcile contradictions.
- Document escalation for unresolved identity and unknown evidence.

**Gate:** No customer pilot until every positive or negative claim in scope has an approved exact relationship and provenance; missing relationships remain unknown.

### Phase 2 - Private prototype configuration

- Replace fictional fixtures only with partner-authorised records in a private environment.
- Apply partner-approved customer language, support links, retention rules, and brand treatment.
- Test current, stale, contradictory, malformed, missing, cross-brand, registry-failure, AI-failure, and print scenarios.
- Complete security, privacy, accessibility, product-safety/legal, and operational review.

**Gate:** Product, technical, support, compliance, and brand owners approve a limited user test. No production or purchase claim is implied.

### Phase 3 - Observed user test

- Observe at least five recent or realistic upgrade decisions across less-experienced and experienced rack owners, as recommended by the Researcher.
- Compare task completion, correct exact-rack identification, evidence comprehension, support use, unresolved outcomes, and user confidence with the existing route.
- Record confusion and failure; do not coach users past interface problems during the measured task.

**Gate:** Continue only if the guided flow adds observable value over the current matrix, product pages, or support route without encouraging overconfidence.

### Phase 4 - Bounded live pilot

- Release to a controlled share of eligible same-brand traffic for the agreed rack family.
- Keep existing support and product instructions visible.
- Monitor registry retrieval, stale/unknown rate, condition answers, product-handoff eligibility, Guide verification failures, support escalation, and customer complaints.
- Compare compatibility contacts, attachment conversion, and compatibility-coded returns with the agreed baseline where sample size and attribution permit.
- Hold regular record-owner review and an immediate pause route for evidence or safety concerns.

**Gate:** Expand only on measured benefit, maintained evidence quality, acceptable unknown/failure behavior, and formal human approval.

### Phase 5 - Decision

- **Expand:** add another same-brand family only after its registry passes the same audit.
- **Revise:** improve identification, conditions, evidence wording, or support routing when value exists but friction is high.
- **Stop:** retire the pilot if buyer urgency, customer value, data governance, safe operation, or measurable impact is insufficient.

Cross-brand matching, room planning, structural assessment, and automated safety advice are not expansion shortcuts; each would require a separate evidence and product decision.

## 11. Manager handoff

### Proposed launch

Approve a **human-reviewed outreach and discovery phase**, not a market launch. Use the public fictional prototype for invited demonstrations to a small set of UK/Ireland specialist retailers or manufacturers. The ask is a 25-minute evidence review followed, where warranted, by a one-family data-readiness audit and bounded pilot proposal.

No external outreach, publication change, partner branding, registry edit, spend, integration, deployment, or live pilot should occur without explicit human approval. The current public prototype may be demonstrated at its verified URL with the disclaimers and script in this plan.

### Dependencies

- Human approval of target list, sender identity, outreach wording, and demonstration use.
- Buyer access across ecommerce/commercial, support, catalogue/PIM, technical product, and compliance ownership.
- Partner-authorised exact-version compatibility data and commerce links.
- Named record owner, approval workflow, review cadence, contradiction handling, and incident pause process.
- Baseline support, return, and conversion measures with agreed definitions.
- Specialist legal, product-safety, privacy/security, accessibility, and brand review before customer-facing production use.
- Private configuration, monitoring, registry administration, support routing, and operational ownership for any pilot.

### Principal risks

- Same-brand matrices may already solve the customer job well enough that a guided advisor adds little value.
- Buyer pain and commercial magnitude are not established for UK/Ireland sellers.
- Exact legacy identities or approved relationships may be incomplete, inconsistent, or expensive to govern.
- Customers may overread “confirmed” as an installation or safety assurance despite scope language.
- A public Sheet is suitable for the demonstrator but not automatically an appropriate production governance boundary.
- Sparse CSV rows and date coercion caused a verified import incident; source-contract testing and owner review remain necessary.
- AI or polished presentation may create misplaced confidence unless deterministic authority, provenance, refusal, and limitations stay prominent.
- A broad catalogue rollout could magnify stale records, unsupported pairings, support burden, and reputational risk.

### Measures of success

**Discovery:** proportion of contacted sellers that confirm a material compatibility workflow; ability to name a data owner; access to baseline taxonomy; selection of one viable family.

**Data readiness:** proportion of in-scope exact pairings with approved provenance; stale, contradictory, malformed, and missing relationship rates; time and ownership required to resolve unknowns.

**Customer task:** exact-rack identification completion; decision-sheet completion; comprehension of the four states and conditions; unresolved/escalation rate; print/save or support-summary use.

**Commercial/operational:** eligible attachment handoff and conversion; compatibility-related contacts per eligible session/order; handling time; compatibility-coded cancellations and returns. Report only with baseline, sample, period, and attribution caveats.

**Reliability/governance:** successful live retrieval rate; fail-closed events; Guide verification and availability failures; review-due compliance; record incident count and resolution time; support escalations and complaints.

**Decision rule:** Recommend expansion only when the pilot shows customer or operational value against the agreed baseline, evidence ownership remains sustainable, unresolved behavior is safe and useful, and all responsible human owners approve. Otherwise revise or stop.

### Core message for the Purple Manager

Northstar should be communicated as a **governed decision-layer demonstrator**, not an AI compatibility oracle. Its strongest buyer story is that it preserves a retailer's authority while making exact same-brand upgrade evidence easier to act on. The public build proves the interaction and evidence controls with fictional data; only a partner pilot can prove commercial value.
