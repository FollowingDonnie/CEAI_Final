# Deep Research Prompt

You are producing an independent evidence dossier for a separate AI Researcher working inside a five-agent product-development organisation. Your report is an input to that agent, not the final opportunity decision.

## Context

The founder experienced difficulty while building a home gym. Equipment dimensions, operating clearances, technical specifications, and attachment compatibility were often incomplete, inconsistent, or difficult to compare. The founder suspects that an AI-enabled service could help consumers and might also create value for gym-equipment retailers or manufacturers.

Mirafit was mentioned by the founder as one possible research lead because its product information appeared comparatively detailed. This is only a hypothesis. Do not assume Mirafit is the right company, that a retailer-specific solution is best, that the final product should be a chatbot, or that the founder's preferred interpretation is correct.

The intended outcome of the wider project is a convincing public prototype that demonstrates what is possible to gym-equipment companies and uses at least one live external data source at runtime. The runtime should not attempt to retrieve the unrestricted internet.

Primary commercial context: the UK and Ireland. Include international evidence and analogues where they materially improve the analysis.

## Research objective

Determine whether there is a credible, commercially useful opportunity around AI-supported home-gym planning, equipment selection, specification comparison, or compatibility checking. Identify the strongest problem, audience, buyer, product direction, and defensible live-data strategy. Actively search for evidence that contradicts the idea as well as evidence that supports it.

## Questions to investigate

1. How common and consequential are problems involving home-gym equipment dimensions, room fit, operating clearance, rack ecosystems, and attachment compatibility?
2. Which groups experience the greatest difficulty: first-time home-gym buyers, experienced lifters, personal trainers, gym designers, retailers, manufacturers, customer-support teams, or another segment?
3. What do people currently do to solve these problems? Include forums, spreadsheets, comparison guides, retailer support, configurators, room planners, compatibility databases, and general AI tools.
4. What existing products, services, configurators, recommendation tools, or commercial analogues address parts of the problem? Identify their strengths, limitations, pricing where relevant, and unserved gaps.
5. Who could pay for a solution and why? Compare consumer, retailer, manufacturer, affiliate, lead-generation, software-as-a-service, and white-label models.
6. What measurable value might a gym-equipment company receive, such as improved conversion, larger baskets, more appropriate cross-selling, reduced pre-sales support, fewer incompatible purchases, or fewer returns? Do not assert benefits without evidence.
7. Which equipment companies provide sufficiently detailed and accessible product information? Examine several companies rather than centring the analysis on Mirafit.
8. What live-data approaches are realistically available? Compare official APIs, affiliate or merchant feeds, Shopify or WooCommerce interfaces, product-information-management exports, Google Sheets, controlled databases, public datasets, user-supplied specifications, and carefully bounded public-page retrieval.
9. What access, reliability, licensing, copyright, robots, terms-of-use, privacy, safety, and product-liability risks would affect a demonstrator or later commercial deployment?
10. Which parts of compatibility can be determined from structured rules and measurements, and which require explicit manufacturer confirmation or human review?
11. What alternative opportunities in the wider gym-equipment domain might be stronger than the founder's initial idea?
12. Which three to five opportunity directions deserve consideration, and which one appears strongest based on evidence rather than novelty alone?

## Evidence requirements

- Prioritise primary sources, official company documentation, public technical specifications, reputable market research, and recent evidence from 2024-2026.
- Use older sources only when they remain authoritative or provide important historical context.
- Use customer discussions, reviews, and forums to identify language and pain points, but label anecdotal evidence clearly.
- Include working links, publication dates, and access dates.
- Cite every material statistic, quotation, market claim, product capability, price, and technical assertion inline.
- Distinguish verified fact, expert opinion, customer anecdote, and your own inference.
- Do not fabricate missing figures or treat search-result snippets as evidence.
- Where credible sources disagree, present the disagreement.
- Include evidence against the opportunity and conditions under which it should be rejected.

## Required report structure

1. Executive summary
2. Research scope and method
3. Problem validation and contrary evidence
4. Audience and buyer analysis
5. Current behaviours and customer pain points
6. Competitor and analogue landscape
7. Gym-equipment company and data-source landscape
8. Live-data options comparison
9. Commercial models and value hypotheses
10. Technical, legal, ethical, safety, and trust risks
11. Three to five opportunity directions, scored against desirability, feasibility, viability, differentiation, data access, and prototype suitability
12. Recommended direction, confidence level, and reasons
13. Assumptions and unresolved questions for the Researcher to investigate independently
14. Evidence that could invalidate the recommendation
15. Complete numbered source register

Do not design the final interface, select the implementation stack, write code, or produce marketing copy. Deliver an evidence-rich research dossier that another Researcher can audit and use as one input among several.
